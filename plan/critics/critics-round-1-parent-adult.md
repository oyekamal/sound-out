# Critics round 1: parent and adult (plan-v1.md)

Read plan-v1.md and bar/*.json (Khan 201 MB, 4.80 on 131k ratings; ABC 212 MB, 4.25 on 3.8k ratings, last updated 2023). WebFetch of both Common Sense reviews worked. ABC: ages 3+, forced sequential path, "mini-games can sometimes feel like drills", mic and child name at setup. Khan: ages 3+, email-verified parent account, limited parent tracking, mic needed for full function. Judged from the plan; nothing was run.

## Verdicts after one week

**Person 1, the mother (3 GB phone, two kids 5 and 8, Khan Kids and ABC already installed): B.** Both bar apps already give her kids characters, songs, books and games she can hand over and walk away from; Option A needs her to sit and judge English phonics she cannot hear, and it adds nothing her kids ask for.

**Person 2, the shopkeeper: A, conditionally.** B is dead for him (Duolingo is built for people who can already read the menu in the language they know and I have already quit it), while A is the only thing here that starts from "cannot read a letter" and hides from his customers, but only if night one is fixed (gap below).

## Single biggest gap

**Night one, before the first lesson: Plan 3.4 and 3.13 put an English-language test in front of a man who cannot read English.** The path is language grid, "who is reading", mic question, nickname, demo, then placement: Stage 0 spoken yes/no questions ("Do you know any English?", "Have languages always felt hard?"), Stage 1 tap-the-picture sound games, then Stage 2, 26 letters, where the plan's own example (Rukhsana, 14/26) fails twelve letters in a row. That is easily 10 to 15 minutes of a 15-minute night, in a shop, with his phone speaking questions aloud about his difficulty, before he has learned one thing. The moment he stops: the second spoken question, or the first letter he misses with nobody to say it is fine. Stage 1 also assumes English picture vocabulary ("tap the one that starts with /s/": sun, soap, saw). If he does not know the English word for the picture, the task is not measuring sound awareness and he fails for the wrong reason. Better apps: ABC starts at letter one; Duolingo puts you in a lesson within a minute. Verify: stopwatch a first-time Urdu-only non-reader on a 2 GB phone; pass = hearing and tapping a first letter-sound inside 90 seconds with zero spoken questions about his history, and placement inferred from the first lesson (start at letter 1, fast-forward on streaks of correct answers).

## Further defects

**1. Helper dependency (3.9, 3.4 Stage 3, 4.8).** The helper must pass a qualification: five audio examples with planted errors ("tuh" vowel added, "aaa" for short a). A mother who does not read English cannot hear these. Failure path is unspecified; and 90% real-and-pseudoword gates "never promote a learner on its own" (section 0). So the child cannot be promoted past the first gate by a home with no English reader. Khan and ABC need no adult judgement. Fix: a "no judge at home" mode where gates are passed by tap, tile and spelling (the plan already says E6b exists) and are labelled "checked by the app", not "provisional". Verify: run a parent who reads no English through the qualification and then through three gate attempts; the child must reach lesson 2.x without the word "provisional" appearing.

**2. Role question (3.13 step 2, 7.4).** "I am reading" versus "My child is reading. I will help" is spoken plus icon plus text. For a non-reader the two icons (one person, adult plus small figure) are the only cue, and the second card is singular but she has two kids, 5 and 8, who need different tracks and different placements, and the 8-year-old will tap through "Grown-ups: tap the number seven" because it is spoken aloud. A shopkeeper who sees a small figure may feel the app is a kids' app.  Verify: five Urdu-only adults pick a card from the icons alone with the sound off; 5/5 correct. Test that the 8-year-old cannot pass the gate by listening.

**3. Shared-phone profiles (6.5, 3.1).** The profile picker is the launch screen, so the kids' launch screen shows the shopkeeper's profile name and the "Track B" skin when the next person opens the app, and switching to Track B needs no gate, so an 8-year-old can enter, or delete progress in, the adult profile at will on a shared phone. Fix: profile picker shows avatars only, adult profile PIN-optional and hidden from the child list by default. Verify: hand the phone to an 8-year-old and have them find and open the adult profile in under a minute; it must fail.

**4. Child says the wrong sound, parent is cooking (3.9, 5.1, 5.5).** Listening "confirms but never fails". So when the child says /sss/ wrong, what does the app do? Plan: for helper-judged items the screen waits for "Got it"/"Not yet" from an adult who is at the stove; for Track A the machine stays silent. Result either the child is stuck on a button waiting, or taps "Got it" themself (the plan gives no lock). ABC and Khan respond on their own and move on. Fix: after a timeout the item auto-defers into tomorrow's review, with no adult required to keep the session moving. Verify: a 5-year-old session with the parent absent for 6 minutes; the child must finish six screens, with every unjudged item marked pending and re-queued, never auto-passed.

**5. Voice-only navigation (7.4).** "Every control has an icon and a spoken label" is a rule, not a design: spoken on tap means a non-reader must tap everything to find out what it does, including Delete. The plan has no test for destructive actions. Punjabi is "served by the Urdu UI first", so Punjabi speakers get Urdu, not Punjabi. Better apps: Duolingo ABC auto-plays a voice instruction on every screen for pre-readers. Verify: run an audit with the screen reader off and a blindfold on the text: complete lesson one, then restore a backup, using only audio; any step needing text fails.

**6. 27 MB is true for a quarter, not for the app (4.1).** Base 27 MB covers L0-L2 only. Full audio is about 75 MB, packs L3-L7 add roughly 54 MB, and the optional listening model adds 40-123 MB: up to roughly 200 MB, the same as the 201 and 212 MB bar apps. The plan's headline "The bar apps are 96-212 MB" is placed beside 27 MB with no mention of the rest. Packs download "on Wi-Fi by default"; the shopkeeper has no Wi-Fi in the shop, and the pack arrives after months of study at the plan's own pace (about 8-9 weeks per level), so the first sign of the cost is a stuck lesson. The plan does not say what screen shows when the pack is missing. I cannot verify current PKR data prices; the plan states none. Verify: the store listing and in-app size screen both show total size per level in MB and an estimate in rupees; airplane-mode test at the L2 to L3 boundary shows a spoken, icon-led "downloading needs internet" message and still allows review.

Not checked: real APK size, cold start and Opus playback on a 3 GB phone; whether the bar apps work offline today.
