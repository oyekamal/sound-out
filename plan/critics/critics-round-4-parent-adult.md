Read: plan-v4.md in full; bar/Khan_Academy_Kids.json and Learn_to_Read_Duolingo_ABC.json; Common Sense reviews of both (fetched 2026-10-06). Khan: parent email verified before kid accounts, path personalised by age and past performance. Duolingo ABC: child's name plus mic access, "kids can't choose where to start", no skipping ahead.

## Verdicts after one week

**Mother: B.** Sound Out gives my 5-year-old one 8-minute sitting and a nearly empty Stories tab, while Khan Kids has 5,000 things she can open alone.

**Shopkeeper: A.** Only if night one really ends with him reading "sat"; Duolingo is the app he already quit.

## The single biggest stop

**Day 2 or 3, evening: Ayesha finishes Sitting B in about 7 minutes, gets her sticker, and asks for more.** The plan never says what happens next (§3.2, §3.4).
- If the next sitting waits until tomorrow, there is nothing for her: the village has 2 pieces, the chant lights 3 letters, and Stories holds at most one English passage she cannot understand (the plan does not say whether passages unlock with lessons or all show at once).
- If sittings run back to back, she spends forty minutes on s, a, t.

She cannot read, so Practice (1-3 cards) is not hers to start. Her mother hands her Khan Kids and it stays.

## Defects (6)

**1. §3.1 first launch, profiles, shape pattern.**
- Moment: mother taps "A child" and the lesson starts. There is no name, no pattern, one profile, so no doors (§3.1: doors only with 2+ profiles). Hamza (8) plays inside Ayesha's profile; his taps count as her first attempts and unlock her lessons. Or Hamza does the quick-start first and the shared profile lands at L2.03. Later, a 5-year-old cannot recall 4 shapes of 9, so the mother stands there every time or sets none; an 8-year-old who watched four taps knows it.
- Better apps: Khan has the parent create child accounts with name, avatar and age before play; Duolingo ABC asks for the child's name.
- Verify: fresh install, two kids, one phone. Can the mother tell which child the first quick-start is for? Where is "add a child"? Run the sibling test with the pattern unset.

**2. §4.1, §3.12 what she is told about size and data.**
- Moment: "44-59 MB" is an estimate until week 8. Nothing at install says 29-44 MB of packs follow at Level 3. She meets that months later behind a "7 + 8" gate, with "about 3 minutes" computed on an unmeasured 1 Mbit/s (D36) and no rupee figure. On patchy data, 3 minutes may be 15.
- Better apps: Khan and Duolingo ABC are 200+ MiB, but all of it is up front; Khan also lets parents download books and games for offline.
- Verify: add an install-time line "later: about 30 MB more, once". Time a pack at 200 kbps with three drops. Check that a failed pack at Level 3 leads to a screen that says what to do.

**3. §3.5 quick-start for the 8-year-old.**
- Moment: 5 items with no feedback, a 5-minute ladder, then L2.03 with the same Pebble, stickers and bubbles as his sister. The plan does not say what his village shows for skipped letters, or how the 24 L1 and 29 L2 tricky words he skipped are taught, yet Read-it texts use them. Day 3: "this is for Ayesha".
- Better apps: Khan runs to age 8 and adapts by age and performance. Duolingo ABC has no skip-ahead, which Common Sense names as its flaw; here only that is matched.
- Verify: record a placed run and check the first three texts for untaught tricky words. Ask three 8-year-olds if Track A is for babies.

**4. §3.11 mother's view, §3.6 "Needs a person".**
- Moment: "ask her these 3 sounds" works as audio, but she cannot tell /æ/ from /ɑ/ or /p/ from /b/ by ear; she can only compare against the button. `still_learning` then shows "Needs a person". She is the only person in the house. The promise says "no one who reads English has to help", yet the helper, "mastered" and the judge's card all need someone who hears English. She has no step to take.
- Better apps: Khan gives parents a light progress view and no "get a person" state.
- Verify: in the mother test, after "Needs a person" ask "what do you do tonight?" If she says nothing, replace the label with an action (replay these two sounds with her).

**5. §3.5, §3.3 shopkeeper nights 1 to 3.**
- Night one: the course allots 35 minutes to A-C; the app "targets about 15", untimed until week 3. Urdu audio plays on every L1-2 screen, tracing is on by default, then come "Keep going?", the E6d on "sat", and the PIN screen ("one Later") at the moment he is done. Fail that and the promise becomes "your first word on night two".
- Night two is D: "listen" is English passage audio he cannot follow, with an Urdu gloss. Night three is his first "Show what you know": 8 taps (3 real, 5 made-up: tas, ast, sta, sas, att), 3 dictations, bar 9/11, four spoken options each, a 12 s window. A timeout is "not judged" and the check needs 11 judged items, but L1.02 "allows no fresh sets", so it cannot re-ask. After 2 timeouts: "let's finish this tomorrow". For a slow reader that is a loop. "Words you can read now" shows 3 words.
- Better apps: Duolingo's pull is daily reminders and visible progress; the plan removes both (reminders opt-in, weekly).
- Verify: stopwatch five adults on night one including the PIN screen. Run a decoder at 14 s against the L1.02 check and see whether it can finish. Make his first check real words only.

**6. §3.1 Track B, §8 cut list.**
- Moment: C12 lists ~315 pictures once, with no adult set, so Listen & Talk art after each chunk is child-style for a 38-year-old. "Sticker and celebration" is described only for Track A (§3.4); Track B's reward is unspecified. "Track B real-world unlockables", the one adult-coded motive, is cut-list item 4.
- Better apps: Learning Upgrade, the plan's own adult bar, is built for adults (3.2/5 per the plan).
- Verify: Track B screenshot audit in week 3, not week 8, over every L1 Listen & Talk screen. Move the unlockables off the cut list.
