# Critics round 2: parent + adult (plan-v2.md)

Read: plan-v2.md in full, the bar JSONs, both Common Sense reviews.

## Verdicts after one week

- **Mother: B stays.** Khan Kids and ABC give both kids something to do tonight without her help; A gives the 5-year-old one letter sound a day, makes her 8-year-old repeat the same thing, and can only tell her "Checked by the app".
- **Shopkeeper: B (main Duolingo) stays.** After four nights A has taught him about four sounds and no word he could show his daughter; Duolingo at least hands him a word in the first minute, which is why he stays long enough to quit it again.

## The single biggest stop in week 1: Sitting B, the first time the child must speak (day 2)

Plan sections 3.3 (E5), 5.4, 5.5, 3.5.

L1.02 Sitting A is hear, meet, trace, mini (one sound, s). Sitting B is the first one that needs the microphone. The 5-year-old gets: an English Android permission dialog her mother cannot read, possibly a "Listening needs a download, N MB" (verifier model, 5 to 40 MB, section 4.1) on mobile data, then a mic ring in a room with a TV and an 8-year-old. She says "sat". The plan's own recall bar is `clear_yes` recall of 60%, so up to 4 in 10 correct answers can come back `unsure`. `unsure` and `clear_no` look identical: Repair 1 replays the sounds, Repair 2 models the first sound, she speaks again, then the item "moves on" and returns two items later. A child who said the right word gets sent back twice, silently, with no sentence like "I could not hear you". Three `unsure` in a row make the item `pending` and re-queue it (section 5.4).

Where it breaks: the state table (section 3.5) sends a lesson to `provisional` only for "no mic, permission denied, or the cut path". A working mic that keeps saying `unsure` is in no row. The check never reaches `awaiting_recheck`, never reteaches, never parks (parking needs a third *miss*, not a third *pending*). The child meets the same item daily, the grown-up page has no "pending" or "unsure rate" flag (section 3.11 lists parked gates, repeated on-ramp, screener, letter recall), and the mother, who cannot judge English, has no way to see why her daughter is stuck. Khan and ABC accept the tap and move on; the cost of their leniency is credit, the cost of ours is a dead end at day 2.

Verify: put the build on a 3 GB phone in a real room (TV on, sibling talking) with five real 5-year-olds who say the target correctly. Count (a) repairs shown to correct answers, (b) days until L1.02 reaches `secure` or `provisional`, (c) whether the mother can say in Urdu what is wrong from the grown-up page alone. Fail if any child is still `pending` after three sittings with no visible flag.

## Further defects

**1. Shopkeeper, relaunch on night two (section 3.1, 3.4, 1.1).** He taps "Me", gets the lesson, closes it. Next open: a launch screen of child avatars first, his own profile hidden behind a lock tile that needs a grown-up PIN. He is looking at cartoon children, in the shop, with customers able to see the screen, and a PIN he never made. Better apps: Duolingo opens straight to his path. Verify: fresh install as "Me", close, reopen; a non-reader must reach his lesson in 10 s with no PIN screen.

**2. Shopkeeper, 15 minutes, no word (section 1.1, 2.3 rule 1, 3.4).** Track B night one is Sitting A: hear s, meet s, trace s, one mini-check. Section 1.2 projects about 16 sittings before he reads anything at L1.06. Rule 1 bans pictures beside undecoded words, so there is no "apple" or "shop" to win with. "Words you can read now" is empty for weeks. Urdu instructions auto-play aloud with no headphones prompt. Better apps: Duolingo gives a word with a picture in minute one. Verify: after night one, ask him to read one English word and say what he would tell his family; ask three adults if they would open the app on night three. Consider a decoded word on night one (sat) in Sitting A, or an honest in-app roadmap in Urdu saying "first word tomorrow".

**3. The 8-year-old and the 5-year-old's profile (section 3.1, 3.4, 3.5, 6.8 test 8).** Child tiles open with no PIN; the only enforced test is that a child cannot open an *adult* profile. The 8-year-old can re-place his sister at L2.03, skip her gates by tapping through E6c to `provisional`, or speak the answers into her mic ("more than one speaker" pre-gate does not stop a sibling speaking alone). The first profile is also created unnamed at the role screen; whoever taps "stop here" names it, with no PIN existing yet. Khan's accounts sit under a parent login. Verify: extend test 8 to child-versus-child: a 9-year-old gets 10 minutes on a 5-year-old's profile and must not change placement, skip a gate, or earn `secure`.

**4. Install and data honesty (sections 4.1, 7.6, 5.2).** The "54 to 94 MB" is an estimate; the base audio line is labelled a lower bound and the word count has a 60% spread (5,763 to 9,238). The store line quotes N MB for the install, but the verifier model (5 to 40 MB) is a second download at Sitting B if over 25 MB, and level packs (about 54 MB) come later; the mother sees them as separate asks, "show MB" with no rupee figure, on patchy data, and does not know what an MB costs her. No Android sizes for Khan or ABC were checked. Verify: install on a 3 GB phone that already has Khan and ABC, record bundletool size, free storage, total data used from install to the first `secure` lesson, and whether Android kills the app when the verifier loads (budget 300 MB).
