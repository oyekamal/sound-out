# Character research, round 1

The character is the teacher's body. The voice is River (calm, warm). The character listens, speaks, points, praises, corrects gently, waits. Bar: the Duolingo ABC cast (Play: com.duolingo.literacy). Store screenshots are saved locally in `bar/` (gitignored, evaluation only).

## What makes the Duolingo ABC cast work (read off its 10 store screenshots)

- **Shape language.** Every character is one big rounded mass (owl = an egg, bear = a rounded block, monkey = a circle with a face disc). Limbs are small and stubby. Nothing is sharp, so nothing reads as a threat.
- **Silhouette.** Each animal reads in black fill: the owl's ear tufts, the bear's round ears and broad shoulders, the monkey's side ears. A 48 px version still tells them apart.
- **Eyes do the acting.** Large white eyes with dark pupils and a highlight, set wide and slightly oversized. Pupils shift to look at the thing that matters (a letter, the book). The mouth stays simple, and the eyes carry the emotion.
- **Palette.** Flat, saturated, one dominant hue per character (owl green, bear brown, dog blue, monster pink) on equally saturated tile backgrounds (blue, red, purple, green, yellow, orange). No outlines, one darker shade for volume. Colour is the first identifier.
- **Acting out instructions.** The cast is shown doing the verb: reading a book, swinging on vines, holding the tracing pen, hugging a stack of books, peeking in from the edge of the frame pointing at the letter. The character is always next to the task, never replacing it.
- **Cast, not a mascot.** A family of species keeps a screen from feeling empty and lets each learner pick an avatar (dog, monster, mouse in the "who's learning" screen). Duo stays the brand and the teacher.
- **Adults.** Duolingo's adult product keeps the same owl but with less baby framing: the same shapes, copy that is dry and plain, no sparkles on every tap. The cue is restraint, not a different character.

## What must hold across the world and across ages

- **Culture-neutral.** No clothes, hats, skin tone, national animal or religious reference. Animals carry local meaning in places (pig, dog, some birds). Choose animals with no strong taboo or luck meaning anywhere, or a non-animal.
- **No faces that must be read as one gender.** The voice is River; the body should not fight it.
- **Age.** A five-year-old wants round, soft, big-eyed. A forty-year-old rejects primary colours, bouncing, squeaky praise, and a body that is a toy. Fix: calm eyes (not huge), a closed small smile, muted-warm palette with one accent, acts with stillness. Praise is a nod and a soft sparkle, not a jump, for Track B. The same body, quieter motion.
- **Instructions without text.** Six verbs the character must do: speak, listen, point, cheer, wait, correct. Each needs an arm or limb to point and a head angle to listen. A character without arms (the lamp, the cloud) must grow hands or a tuft.
- **Tiny size.** It must read at 48 px as an icon: one colour mass, two eyes, one unique silhouette feature.
- **Never print text.** Image generators draw fake letters; every output is checked for them.

## Concept filters used in round 1

Each concept had to differ in species or form, have an easy two-syllable name, work for both tracks, hold a recognisable silhouette at 48 px, and avoid everything in the "taken" list.
